

>[!NOTE]
>
>This document explains the purpose, structure, and functioning of the Data Entry Team (DET) during TOTs. It outlines the challenges before DET formation and how DETs improve reporting efficiency, map accuracy, and field operations.

---

## 1. What is a Data Entry Team (DET)?

Data Entry Teams were introduced to assist with reporting tasks, primarily for entering data (Church Plant track) and providing updated maps to Pauls during TOTs.

Initially, this was their main responsibility, but over time their role expanded to include ACHIEVE-related tasks such as:

- Adding or correcting villages  
- Managing target areas  
- Handling surveys  

The first test of DET effectiveness was conducted in **October 2024**.

---

## 2. Why was DET needed (Problems before DET)

Previously, during TOTs the reporting team collected reports (Paul reports, surveys, UPG) and entered them into Excel. These files were sent to the Reporting Manager/Coordinator after or immediately following the TOT.

The Reporting Coordinator/Manager then checked for issues such as:

1. Timothy/Paul name mismatches  
2. Baptisms greater than NB  
3. 2G reported but no Titus  
4. Unusually high numbers of orphans/widows  
5. Missing or incorrect village names  

---

### The Challenge with This Approach

1. **Outdated Maps for Pauls**

   Pauls were not receiving updated maps during TOTs. Without uploading surveys and CP track data (which Pauls bring as their past 3-month report) into the software, map updates cannot occur.

   As a result, Pauls were often given **outdated maps**, reducing effectiveness in church planting (red/yellow zones).

2. **Technical Delay in iShare**

   iShare typically takes **24–48 hours** to update map colors after data entry.

   If data was not entered on **Day 1**, the opportunity to provide updated maps during the TOT was often lost, since TOTs last only **three days**.

3. **Slow Printing Infrastructure**

   Printers used during TOTs were often **old and slow**, making it difficult to print maps efficiently.

4. **Post-TOT Error Correction Difficulties**

   Fixing errors after TOTs was difficult because contacting MTs, ZLs, and Pauls was time-consuming.

   Bulk uploads often caused mismatches between:

   - *Total Churches Reported*
   - *Church Records Created*

   These issues could usually only be corrected at the **next TOT**, creating a repetitive cycle.

   With **ACHIEVE tool integration**, these problems would become even more complex.

5. **FR Visit Scheduling Delays**

   Because reporting teams sometimes delayed uploading church records, **Field Representatives (FRs)** could not schedule their visits.

   FRs had to wait until the church records were uploaded before planning fellowship visits.

---

## 3. How Does Forming a DET Solve the Problem?

Previously, **one reporting member handled all reporting tasks during TOTs**, which was ineffective.

Forming a **team of at least four members** (for TOTs with ~45 Pauls) resolves these issues.

The process works as follows:

- **Day 1:** Initial data entry and verification  
- **Day 2:** Continued updates and corrections  
- **Day 3:** Final checks and map printing  

> [!IMPORTANT]
> One member of the DET should belong to the reporting team and be well trained in DET processes and ACHIEVE for the team to be effective.

> [!TIP]
> Refer to the detailed flowchart to better understand the working process of a DET during a TOT.

---

## 4. Who Are the Members of a Data Entry Team?

DET members may include:

- Field Representatives (FRs)
- Reporting team personnel  
  - Administrators  
  - Coordinators  
  - Field Assistants  
  - Managers  

---

## 5. Who Decides the Members for DET?

Reporting Managers and FR Managers jointly decide:

- Whether DETs are required  
- Number of DET teams (varies by country)  
- Number of members per team  
- Training schedules and training content  
- Resources required (laptops, printers)

> [!NOTE]
> Both departments must agree before finalizing and informing members.

---

## 6. Minimum Requirements for DET Members

DET members are expected to have:

- Proficiency in using laptops  
- Basic knowledge of **Microsoft Excel and Word**  
- Basic knowledge of **iMetrics, iShare, Sparrow, Falcon, and Process**  
- Basic understanding of **ACHIEVE and TTI**  
- Curiosity and willingness to learn  

> [!TIP]
> Newly joined FRs are not encouraged to join DET immediately. They should first gain exposure to TOT teachings and departmental responsibilities.

---

## 7. Do We Provide Laptops to Every DET Member?

No.

Each DET typically has **four members**, with responsibilities divided as follows:

- **Two members**
  - Survey entry
  - Church Plant data entry
  - Adding missing villages

- **Two members**
  - Data corrections
  - Error rectification

This division of responsibilities ensures faster task completion.

---

## 8. When Does a DET Operate?

Currently, DETs function **only during TOTs** because FRs are available at that time (they do not travel during TOTs).

All DET tasks must be completed **during the TOT itself**. Leaving tasks for later defeats the purpose of forming a DET.

---

## 9. Do FRs (Who Are Part of DET) Help After TOTs?

No.

FRs who assist as part of DET **only support during TOTs**, since they resume their own field responsibilities afterward.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Reporting managers must ensure all DET responsibilities are completed during the TOT and that no pending items remain.

---

## 10. Who Oversees DETs During TOTs?

The **reporting team member present at the TOT** (who is also part of the DET) is responsible for:

- Monitoring DET performance
- Ensuring reporting goals are achieved

> [!NOTE]
> FRs are not held accountable for DET performance. Their role is to assist the reporting team.
