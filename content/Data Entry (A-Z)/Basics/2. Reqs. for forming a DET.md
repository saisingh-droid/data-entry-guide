

> [!NOTE]
> These are some of the key points that should be considered before or during the formation of a country’s Data Entry Team (DET). While these represent the primary considerations, they are not exhaustive. Please feel free to adapt and expand upon them based on the specific needs of your country.

---

## 1. Adequate Team Strength

Ensure there are sufficient Data Entry Team (DET) members based on operational requirements.

For example, if two TOTs are conducted simultaneously at different locations, a minimum of approximately **eight members** would be required.

(This is an estimation based on ~45 Pauls in a TOT. Approximations may vary.)

---

## 2. Advance Training of Team Members

Selected DET members must undergo training well in advance—ideally at least **three months prior**—to ensure they are familiar with the reporting concepts, tools, and workflows.

Training may be conducted **online or in person**, depending on the country context.

Please refer to the **Data Entry Team Training Guidebook** for detailed training guidelines.

---

## 3. Availability of Required Resources

Confirm that the country has the necessary infrastructure and equipment, such as:

- Laptops  
- Printers  

Resource requirements may vary by country and should be assessed accordingly.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Reporting and FR Managers are required to follow the appropriate procedures and channels to acquire resources and assets  
> *(i.e., analysis → budgeting → communication → procurement → distribution → training).*

---

## 4. Ongoing Communication and Feedback

Maintain consistent communication, provide timely updates, and establish a structured feedback mechanism to ensure the Data Entry Team operates effectively and efficiently.

> [!TIP]
> This is a critical requirement, as processes and tools continue to evolve. Therefore, updated information (including processes, forms, and training materials) must be communicated to DET members to ensure alignment and consistency across the team.
